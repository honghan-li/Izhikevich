  whether the integrator is a fused kernel or a sequence of array ops, and what

  One thread per parameter set; the entire 250k-step DP5 loop lives inside one

  Answering "how long for a reasonable number of spikes": the binding constraint
  is the slowest rhythm, not the fastest. Burst envelopes are built from u
  accumulating on the 1/a timescale, so at a=0.005 the interburst period can
  reach several hundred ms. A 2000 ms window gives ≥8 cycles at 4 Hz, ≥20 at 10
  Hz, hundreds at FS rates, and 2–4 cycles at the very slowest corner.
  Spike-count floor for a meaningful spectrum is ~8 spikes, which at 2000 ms is
  a 4 Hz firing-rate floor — anything below that is quarantined rather than
  clustered.

  2. Integrator

  Fixed-step DP5 (DOPRI5 tableau, 5th-order solution, FSAL → 6 RHS evals/step).
  Fixed, not adaptive, deliberately: the embedded 4(5) error estimate is
  meaningless on a step containing a reset, since the step has an O(1) jump — a
  step controller would either reject forever or need explicit event location.
  Fixed step with event-at-step-end is the honest SIMD choice.

  Error budget: DP5 truncation on the smooth flow is negligible (|dv/dt| ≤ 330 
  mV/ms at the threshold, error O(dt⁶)). The binding error is spike-time 
  quantisation from the reset, bounded by dt_int = 0.01 ms. At 1000 Hz (top of
  the analysis band) that's a 3.6° phase error; at 100 Hz, 0.36°. In-band
  magnitude error < 0.2%.

  - Peak equalisation: on trigger, the sample is recorded as exactly +30.0
  before resetting v←c, u←u+d — the same thing the paper does in Fig. 3. Without
  it, the overshoot (a step can end at up to ~33 mV) injects
  parameter-dependent amplitude noise straight into the high bins.
  - Per-sample adaptivity, not per-step: the embedded 4th-order estimate is
  computed on non-spike steps and reduced to a per-sample max local error. Any
  sample exceeding 1e-6, or going non-finite, is re-run at dt/4 in a second
  pass. That's the right way to get adaptivity on SIMD hardware.
  - Convergence gate (hard, runs before the sweep): 24 probe vectors (the
  paper's 7 types + 17 LHS points) at dt ∈ {0.04, 0.02, 0.01, 0.005, 0.0025, 
  0.001}; accept 0.01 only if the 128-band feature vector is within 1% relative
  L2 of the dt=0.001 reference. Takes seconds. I'd rather gate than assert.

  Kernel layout: one thread per parameter set, 256 threads/block, v,u and all
  parameters in registers for the whole 250k-step loop. Output written as
  traces[t * n_batch + i] so consecutive threads write consecutive addresses —
  fully coalesced. Chunk = 2048 → 164 MB trace buffer, ~500 MB peak with cuFFT
  workspace, comfortable in 4094 MiB. At 2048 threads on 2048 cores there's no
  latency hiding, so expect ~5–10% of peak FP32 → roughly 1–3 s per chunk. Not
  worth optimising; the sim is not the bottleneck.

  The kernel also accumulates, in registers: spike count, ISI sum/sumsq/min/max,
  burst index, adaptation index, mean/SD of v, max local error, finite flag.
  These are not clustering features — they're what lets us name the clusters
  afterwards.

  3. Feature vector

  Mean-subtract → Hann → rfft → |·| → band-limit to 1000 Hz → 128 bands → log1p
  → L2-normalise.

  One correction to what I showed you earlier: pure log-spacing from 0.5 Hz
  breaks, because a 6.1%-wide band at 0.5 Hz is 0.03 Hz — far narrower than Δf =
  0.5 Hz, leaving ~40 empty bands. So the banding is hybrid: 40 native 0.5 Hz
  bins covering 0.5–20 Hz, then 88 log-spaced bands (4.55%/band) covering
  20–1000 Hz, where band width ≥ Δf holds throughout. Each band value is the RMS
  magnitude over its bins, so wide bands don't win just by containing more of
  them.

  4. Sample counts — answering n and m

  - iteration 1: n₁ = 4096, scrambled LHS (optimization='random-cd'), plus the 
  paper's 7 canonical vectors injected as seeds so the known types are
  guaranteed present.
  - iterations 2–12: n = 2048 each, split 70% GP-acquisition / 30% fresh LHS.
  - m = 12, total N = 26 624.

  m=12 because the GP needs enough rounds for the ARD lengthscales to sharpen
  (below ~8 it barely adapts) while per-round overhead stays affordable. The 30%
  LHS floor is not padding — max-variance acquisition reliably collapses into
  one region once lengthscales go anisotropic, and the floor keeps the design
  globally ergodic so a cluster in an unvisited corner can still be found.

  5. GP sampler

  Unit hypercube (a via log then min-max). Outputs = first 6 PCA components of
  the feature matrix, each standardised. Six independent ARD-RBF GPs + constant
  mean + Gaussian noise, hyperparameters by L-BFGS-B on the log-marginal
  likelihood with analytic gradients. Training cap N_gp = 3000, composed as all
  points from the last two iterations plus a uniform draw from earlier, so
  recent structure is never diluted.

  One mechanism worth stating explicitly, because it's the easiest thing to get
  wrong: GP posterior variance does not depend on the observed y for a fixed 
  kernel. Max-variance acquisition would therefore degenerate into a plain
  space-filling design. It becomes informative only because the ARD lengthscales
  are learned — directions and regions where the spectra change fast get short
  lengthscales and hence high variance. ARD (not isotropic) is mandatory, and
  two y-dependent terms are added:

  Pool of 20 000 scrambled-Sobol candidates, each term rank-normalised:
  - 0.40 × total posterior SD, weighted by PC explained variance → explore
  - 0.30 × ‖∇ₓ μ(x)‖ (analytic for RBF) → seek bifurcation surfaces, where
  behaviour changes fastest
  - 0.30 × predictive entropy of a 25-NN classifier on current HDBSCAN labels →
  seek type boundaries and unlabelled voids

  Batch picked greedily, re-conditioning posterior variance after each pick
  (exact and y-free via rank-1 Cholesky update) — that's what supplies the
  repulsion that stops the batch clumping on one maximum. The per-iteration
  interim clustering that feeds the entropy term is cheap: PCA(50) → HDBSCAN on
  an 8000-point subsample, no UMAP. Full UMAP runs once, at the end.

  6. UMAP and HDBSCAN — answering the dimension question

  Two separate UMAP fits, and they must not be the same one.

  Clustering embedding: n_components = 10.
  - Not the 2D viz embedding: 2D UMAP aggressively distorts local density to fit
  the plane. HDBSCAN is a density algorithm, so clustering there both fragments
  real clusters and fuses adjacent ones. Cluster in 10D, colour the 2D plot
  with those labels.
  - Not 2–5: UMAP has to tear the topology to embed that low, merging types.
  - Not 20–50: HDBSCAN's mutual-reachability distance degrades as the k-NN
  distance distribution concentrates; density contrast collapses above ~15–20
  dims.
  - n_neighbors=30, min_dist=0.0, metric='euclidean', n_epochs=500, 
  random_state=42.
    - min_dist=0.0 specifically because this embedding feeds a density clusterer
  — it lets points pack arbitrarily tightly, maximising density contrast.
  min_dist is otherwise a purely cosmetic parameter.
    - n_neighbors=30: at N≈27k with 10–40 expected clusters (~700–2700 pts
  each), 30 balances local detail against global structure; 15 over-fragments,
  100 washes out small clusters.
    - euclidean on L2-normalised features is monotone-equivalent to cosine, so
  it yields an identical k-NN graph and is faster.
  - PCA(50) before UMAP — retains >99% of the 128 bands' variance, denoises, ~3×
  faster k-NN, and the same basis is reused by the GP sampler.

  Visualisation embedding: n_components=2, n_neighbors=30, min_dist=0.1, fitted
  independently on the same PCA(50) input, coloured by the 10D labels.

  HDBSCAN: min_cluster_size=60 (≈0.22% of N), min_samples=10,
  cluster_selection_method='leaf', prediction_data=True,
  allow_single_cluster=False.
  - min_samples=10 is the key knob — the library default (= min_cluster_size) is
  far too conservative and will label a large fraction as noise while
  swallowing small clusters.
  - 'leaf' is what serves your objective directly. The default 'eom' (excess of
  mass) prefers few large stable clusters and absorbs fine structure; 'leaf'
  returns the finest leaves of the condensed tree. Report both — leaf as the
  fine taxonomy (A, B, C…), eom as a coarse grouping, plus the leaf→eom
  containment table.
  - Sweep: n_neighbors ∈ {15,30,50} × min_cluster_size ∈ {30,60,120} ×
  min_samples ∈ {5,10,25} × method ∈ {leaf,eom} = 54 configs, but UMAP refits
  only 3× (once per n_neighbors). Scored by relative_validity_ (DBCV) with a
  penalty for noise fraction > 25%.

  Quarantine before UMAP: truly-at-rest samples (no spike and in-band AC power
  below a floor) would form one enormous degenerate blob that distorts the whole
  k-NN graph. Hold them out as cluster Z and report their parameter region
  separately. Critically, do not drop all zero-spike samples — subthreshold
  resonators (RZ) have zero spikes but a real spectral peak, and the AC-power
  floor is exactly what separates the two. Expect this to matter, since b < 
  0.101 is inside the box.

❯ Should discrete integration be applied before the DFFT?                       

● Two readings of that, and they land differently. Let me take both.

  Reading A — cumulative integration (cumsum/trapezoid of v) before the rFFT

  No. But the thing it would buy you is worth having, applied elsewhere.

  Time-domain integration is exactly a 1/f spectral weighting. The motivation is
  sound: v(t)'s spectrum is dominated by the spike waveform's broadband
  harmonic comb, and a 1/f tilt suppresses the high harmonics and emphasises the
  slow burst envelope — pushing the features toward rhythm and away from
  waveform. That's a genuinely different metric from what I specified, because
  log-banding changes bin widths, log1p compresses amplitude, and L2 normalises
  scale — none of them apply a frequency-dependent tilt.

  But doing it by integrating in the time domain is the worse way to get it:

  - A cumsum has infinite DC gain, so any residual mean or slow drift becomes a
  ramp that swamps the low bins. We mean-subtract, but the non-stationarity I
  flagged in §9.7 (slow corners not settled by 500 ms) gets amplified by exactly
  the 1/f factor — the worst possible interaction.
  - Integrating before windowing means the Hann window then applies to a signal
  whose endpoints no longer match, reintroducing leakage.
  - It's a fixed, unparameterised tilt. You can't dial it.

  Post-FFT weighting gets the same effect exactly, with none of that: |X(f)| → 
  |X(f)| · (f₀/f)^α. So I'd add --spectral-tilt α with α ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}, default
  0, and sweep it as part of the clustering selection — it's one cheap axis and
  it may well separate burst rhythms better. Note α=1 on log-spaced bands is a
  linear reweighting in log-f, so it's a real change to the UMAP metric, not a
  cosmetic one.

  Reading B — integrating over each output interval instead of point-sampling

  Yes, and this is a gap in §1–2 as I wrote them. I specified decimation 10:1
  (dt_int=0.01 ms → dt_out=0.1 ms) by taking every 10th step, with no anti-alias
  filter. That's wrong, and here it's not a rounding-error concern.

  The reset is a true step discontinuity (v: +30 → c), so the signal is not 
  bandlimited at any sample rate — its harmonics fall only as 1/f. Decimating to
  10 kHz means the 0–1000 Hz band receives folded energy from 9–11 kHz (and
  19–21, …). Putting numbers on it, for a 100 Hz firing rate:

  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────┐
  │                                      │ relative amplitude │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ legitimate content at 1000 Hz (k=10) │ −20 dB             │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ folding content at 9.5 kHz (k=95)    │ −40 dB             │
  └──────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────┘

  So unfiltered, aliasing sits only ~20 dB under the legitimate signal at the
  top of the analysis band — a ~10% amplitude error in the upper bands. Worse
  than the size: the fold amount depends on where the firing rate sits relative
  to 10 kHz, so it's a non-smooth, parameter-dependent perturbation. That's
  corrosive twice over — it adds noise to the UMAP metric, and it violates the
  smooth-response-surface assumption the GP sampler is built on.

  The fix is cheap, and it is precisely "discrete integration before the DFFT":
  emit the mean of the 10 sub-steps rather than the 10th sub-step. That's a
  boxcar decimation filter, and boxcars have nulls exactly at multiples of the
  output rate — which is exactly where the troublesome images live:

  ┌────────────────────────────┬───────────────┬─────────────┐
  │                            │ single boxcar │ cascaded ×2 │
  ├────────────────────────────┼───────────────┼─────────────┤
  │ 9 kHz (worst of fold band) │ −19 dB        │ −38 dB      │
  ├────────────────────────────┼───────────────┼─────────────┤
  │ 10 kHz (image centre)      │ null          │ null        │
  ├────────────────────────────┼───────────────┼─────────────┤
  │ 1 kHz (in-band distortion) │ −0.14 dB      │ −0.28 dB    │
  └────────────────────────────┴───────────────┴─────────────┘

  A single boxcar already gives ~38 dB of margin (≈1% error); cascading twice
  gives ~58 dB (≈0.1%), at roughly two extra FMAs per integration step inside
  the kernel — free. So: cascade ×2.

  And because the filter is a known linear operator, the small in-band tilt is
  removed exactly by dividing the magnitude spectrum by |H(f)|² with H(f) = 
  sin(πfLT)/(L·sin(πfT)), L=10, T=0.01 ms. Residual error is then aliasing
  alone, ~0.1%. Note the tilt is identical for every parameter set, so even
  uncorrected it would be harmless to clustering — but it costs one line to be
  exact.

  One consequence worth stating: the cascaded filter is 19 samples ≈ 0.19 ms
  long, comparable to the ~0.15 ms spike upstroke, so it visibly rounds the
  spikes. That's fine for the 0–1000 Hz features but would look wrong in the
  A1.png trace panels. The re-simulate-representatives design in §8 already
  solves this for free — emit representative traces at full dt_int with the
  filter off, so the displayed spikes stay sharp while the clustered spectra
  stay anti-aliased.

  Spec deltas

  - §2 gains: output stage = cascaded-boxcar (×2, L=10) accumulator in
  registers, emitting at dt_out; representatives re-simulated with filtering
  disabled.
  - §3 gains: divide |X(f)| by |H(f)|² before banding; new --spectral-tilt α ∈ 
  {0, 0.5, 1} applied post-FFT, default 0, added to the selection sweep.
  - §2 convergence gate gains a second check: verify the 128-band vector is
  stable when dt_out is halved to 0.05 ms, which is the direct empirical test
  that aliasing is controlled. If it isn't, the answer is a longer cascade, not
  a smaller dt_int.

  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴───────┴───────┘

  UMAP + HDBSCAN are 74% of the run. The GPU-accelerated part is ~6 min, and the
  Dormand–Prince integration proper — the thing the whole program is nominally
  about — is ~1.4 min of 50. That asymmetry is why §11.1 sizes N by what UMAP
  can chew rather than by what the GPU can simulate.

  So the bottleneck is single-machine CPU clustering. But it decomposes into two
  causes that are both choices, not physical limits:

  Cause 1 — the sweep rebuilds work it doesn't need to (≈11 min recoverable)

  HDBSCAN's expensive phase is the mutual-reachability MST and single-linkage
  tree. That depends on the embedding and on min_samples only. min_cluster_size
  is applied afterwards when condensing the tree, and cluster_selection_method
  later still when extracting clusters from the condensed tree — both cheap.

  My §12.3 stage-1 sweep runs 54 independent HDBSCAN.fit calls, so it rebuilds
  the same tree 6 times over. It should be 9 tree builds (3 embeddings × 3
  min_samples), each followed by 6 near-free extractions: 3 min_cluster_size × 2
  methods.

  9 × 15 s ≈ 2.5 min instead of 13.5 min. The hdbscan package exposes enough to
  do this (single_linkage_tree_ plus _tree_to_labels), though it's a
  semi-internal API and relative_validity_ still costs something per labelling —
  so call it 3–4 min realistically.

  Cause 2 — random_state makes UMAP single-threaded (≈8 min recoverable)

  umap-learn disables parallelism in the layout optimisation when random_state
  is set, to guarantee reproducibility. My §12.1 sets random_state=42 on every
  fit, so all 11 UMAP fits run on 1 of 22 available threads.

  The fix is to be selective about where determinism is actually worth paying
  for:
  - Keep seeded: the one final reported 10D embedding and the 2D visualisation
  embedding. These feed the published figures and the §17 determinism criterion,
  so they must be reproducible.
  - Unseed: the 3 exploratory sweep fits (they only need to be mutually
  comparable within a run, not across runs) and especially the 5 stability fits
  — §13.2 deliberately wants 5 different embeddings, so 5 independent unseeded
  parallel fits are exactly the right thing and are also the fastest.

  I'd want to measure the actual speedup rather than assume it, but even 4× on 9
  of 11 fits takes UMAP from ~18 min to ~11 min.

  A third, smaller lever: I set n_epochs=500 where umap-learn defaults to 200
  for N > 10k. Dropping to 300 would cut layout time ~40%, at some cost to how
  crisply the density structure resolves. I'd treat this as a measured
  trade-off, not a default change.

  Net

  Both fixes together: ~50 min → ~31–34 min, with no reduction in N, trace
  length, sweep coverage, or statistical rigour — it's the same work, just not
  repeated. That also makes the §15 trim levers (--sweep-fast, --compact-csv)
  unnecessary for hitting your 45 min target, and leaves headroom you could
  spend on a larger N instead.

  Worth noting what is not the bottleneck, so it doesn't get optimised by
  reflex: the fused CUDA kernel, the 0.01 ms timestep, the 2500 ms trace length,
  and the GP. Halving dt_int to 0.005 ms would cost under a minute. If
  anything, the budget says we could afford a finer timestep or a longer trace
  than §6 specifies.

  Want me to fold both fixes into §12.3 and §13.2 and re-derive the §15 budget?
  The HDBSCAN tree-reuse one changes an interface (the sweep needs to drive the
  tree and the extraction separately), so it's better in the spec than
  discovered during implementation.

  What changed

  §12.4.1 — tree reuse (new subsection). The sweep now mirrors HDBSCAN's cost
  structure: the MST/single-linkage tree depends on the embedding and
  min_samples only, so stage 1 is 9 tree builds followed by 6 cheap
  condense/extract passes each, instead of 54 full fits. 13.5 → ~3.5 min.
  Because this leans on a semi-internal API (single_linkage_tree_ +
  _tree_to_labels), §17 test 9 asserts reused-tree labellings are identical to
  full fits across all 6 combinations, so a version bump fails loudly rather
  than silently changing clusters. --no-tree-reuse falls back.

  §12.3 — random_state policy (new subsection; old §12.3 became §12.4). Only the
  two reported embeddings are seeded; the 3 sweep fits, 2 tilt fits and 5
  stability fits run unseeded and multi-threaded. The sweep winner is re-fitted
  seeded before anything is reported, so reproducibility of published output is
  preserved. UMAP ~18 → ~8.5 min. §13.2 also gained a note that the ARI figure
  is now a Monte-Carlo estimate, so 0.60 ± 0.03 means re-run, not pass/fail.

  §15 — budget re-derived: ~50 → ~31 min, with a device column. Both savings
  rest on estimates (the ~4× threading ratio, the tree-build/extraction ratio)
  that the manifest must now record as measurements.

  On PCA(50) — yes, and the case is stronger, but one claim was overstated

  It still applies. §12.1 is rewritten with the reasons ordered by actual
  strength:

  1. It's free. §11.2 needs the first 6 PCs every iteration for the GP sampler,
  so the basis is computed regardless. Feeding the same projection to UMAP costs
  nothing.
  2. Denoising. Neighbouring bands of a harmonic comb move together; 50 PCs keep
  >99% of variance while dropping band-to-band noise that would otherwise enter
  the k-NN graph. (whiten=False deliberately — whitening would amplify exactly
  those noise directions.)
  3. Speed — and here I was wrong. I'd written "~3× faster k-NN." That's true of
  the k-NN phase, but misleading as stated: UMAP's layout optimisation runs in
  the output space and is indifferent to input dimension, and at N≈27k the
  layout is the larger share of the fit. Real effect is ~20–30% off a UMAP fit,
  not 3×. The spec now says so explicitly and flags the earlier wording.

  So PCA survives on reasons 1 and 2, not on the speed argument I originally led
  with — which is worth knowing, because it means PCA is not one of the levers
  for the bottleneck. The two restructures above are.

  One residual risk I added a guard for: PCA is linear, so a rare cluster
  separated only along a low-variance direction could be flattened before UMAP
  sees it. §17 test 10 asserts cumulative explained variance at 50 PCs ≥ 99% on
  the real feature matrix, with the instruction to raise n_components rather
  than relax the check.

  There's now ~14 min of headroom against your 45 min target. §15 recommends
  spending it on a larger N rather than banking it — at ~31 min, n = 4096 per
  iteration (N ≈ 53k) would roughly double sample density for maybe +8 min,
  mostly in UMAP. Worth deciding before I write the implementation plan, since N
  propagates into min_cluster_size, N_gp, and the CSV sizes.
